# Games Academy Computer Advice #


**Last updated on 15th September 2026**

We anticipate that you may wish to purchase a new computer to see you through your time at Falmouth University. This page will be updated to reflect our start of term buying advice for students enrolling on our undergraduate courses who wish to purchase a personal computer. 

Depending on your needs you may prefer a desktop or a laptop. We often find our students prefer a laptop due to the portability. Although laptops tend to be more expensive, they do not need additional peripherals.

At Falmouth, you can use industry-standard computers and kit on campus, with some IT suites open 24 hours during teaching weeks.

Computing costs are changing. If you wanted to purchase a personal laptop or desktop with similar specs, you can expect to pay between £300-£1850. This wide range reflects how much cost varies by course and by how new or secondhand you go, so see the course-specific breakdown below for a specific estimate. We also recommend looking at refurbished machines through reputable retailers, such as Currys, Amazon Renewed and Dell Outlet, which is a great way to save costs, and support sustainable practice.

Below, we've separated our recommendations by course, each with a rough specification, price range and list of recommended retailers, covering both off-the-shelf and custom-built systems. These recommendations have been made with our software list's minimum and recommended specs in mind.These take into account latest gen hardware for longevity, though looking at older gen hardware is an easy way to keep costs down without a huge decrease in performance.

# Component price increases warning #
The pricing of various PC components has been enormously volatile for some time now. At the time of writing, there is still a shortage of memory (RAM), storage (SSDs) and graphics cards (GPUs), largely because AI data centres are buying up huge amounts of the world's memory chip supply. This has pushed prices up sharply, and industry analysts don't expect things to properly settle down before 2027. With these uncertainties in mind, we have tried our best to ensure our recommendations below factor in a mix of sensible budget, computing performance and the age/generation of components.

# A quick note on graphics card names #
NVIDIA and AMD graphics cards are named by generation and tier. For NVIDIA, the first two digits (e.g. the "50" in RTX 5070) show the generation, a higher number is newer, so a 50-series card is newer than a 40-series, which is newer than a 30-series. The last two digits show the performance tier within that generation, higher is better (a 5070 outperforms a 5060), and a "Ti" or "Super" suffix is a step up from the base card of the same number. AMD's RX cards follow the same idea with their own numbering. Where we suggest an NVIDIA and an AMD card together (e.g. "RTX 5060 or RX 9060 XT"), they're intended as roughly equivalent options.

## Game Development, Game Animation, Game Art, Computing for Games and Creative Computing, Online Courses

For these courses we recommend a computer that has plenty of power, as students will be working on or with complex 3D assets and game engines, which are notoriously resource heavy.

**Desktop Specifications**

* Rough price range: £1650-£1850
* Processor: AMD Ryzen 5 9600X or Intel Core Ultra 5 245K (current generation)
* Graphics Card: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 or better
* Memory: 16GB DDR5 (32GB is ideal but will push the budget up due to current RAM prices - see component price increases warning above)
* Main Storage: 1TB or higher M.2 NVMe SSD
* Secondary Storage: 1TB or higher SATA SSD
* Operating System: Windows 11

**Budget Desktop Specifications (Older Generation)**

This build uses older, partly secondhand parts instead of the newest hardware - a good way to keep costs down, but the secondhand graphics card won't come with a manufacturer's warranty, and won't have the same lifespan as a current-gen system if you want it to last well beyond graduation.

* Rough price range: £900-£1100
* Processor: AMD Ryzen 5 5600 or Intel Core i5-12400F (older generation)
* Graphics Card: Secondhand NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070, or a newer xx70-tier card or higher
* Memory: 16GB DDR4 (cheaper than DDR5 right now)
* Storage: 1TB M.2 NVMe SSD
* Operating System: Windows 11


**Laptop Specifications**

* Rough price range: £1500-£1800
* Processor: Intel Core Ultra 7 255HX or AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 (current generation)
* Graphics: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 or better
* Memory: 16GB DDR5 (32GB is ideal but will push the budget due to current RAM prices - see component price increases warning above)
* Storage: 1TB or higher M.2 NVMe SSD
* Operating System: Windows 11

**Budget Laptop Specifications (Older Generation)**
This build uses a previous-generation processor and graphics card instead of the newest hardware - a good way to keep costs down, with less headroom for the future than our main recommendation. We don't recommend xx60-tier cards for this course, they don't hold up as well for game development work, so this tier sits closer in price to our optimal laptop than the budget tiers elsewhere in this document.

* Rough price range: £1100-£1450
* Processor: Intel Core i7 (13th/14th Gen) or equivalent AMD Ryzen 7 (older generation)
* Graphics: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070, or a previous-generation RTX 3070
* Memory: 16GB DDR5
* Storage: 512GB-1TB SSD
* Operating System: Windows 11

## Robotics
This course does not require high specs due to the nature of working with embedded systems. Further to this, our robotics students often prefer purchasing a laptop, focusing on portability and battery life due to the mobile nature of the course. A desktop PC is not advised. Many robotics students will purchase second hand laptops and then install a linux distro on them.

**Laptop Specifications**

* Rough price range: £750-£950
* Processor: Intel Core Ultra 5 225U or AMD Ryzen AI 5 340 (current generation)
* Memory: 16GB DDR5 (see component price increases warning above)
* Graphics: Integrated Graphics
* Storage: 512GB SSD
* Operating System: Windows 11 (Most new laptops come with this, Linux can be installed in place of)

**Budget Laptop Specifications (Secondhand)**
Many robotics students buy a secondhand business laptop rather than a new one, then install a Linux distro in place of Windows. Buying from a graded, warrantied refurbisher (see the refurbished retailers recommended above) reduces the risk of doing this compared to an unwarrantied private sale.

* Rough price range: £300-£500
* Processor: Intel Core i5 (10th/11th Gen, e.g. i5-1135G7/i5-1145G7) or AMD Ryzen 5 Pro (5000 series) (older generation, secondhand)
* Memory: 8-16GB DDR4
* Graphics: Integrated Graphics
* Storage: 256GB SSD
* Operating System: Windows 10/11 (often replaced with a Linux distro, as above)
